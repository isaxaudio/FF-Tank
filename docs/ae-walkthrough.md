# FF Tank for account executives: a 20-minute tour

FF Tank is Fatfish's own CRM at https://ff-tank.vercel.app. It works on a laptop and on a phone. You sign in with your
work email and a password. Forgot it? "Forgot password?" on the sign-in page emails you a link.

What it is for, in one line: every conversation with a client lives on the deal, and the app tells you each morning
what needs you first.

## 1. Today (your first screen)

Today lists three kinds of things, each with the owner, the due date and the next action:

- **Replies waiting.** A client wrote back and nobody has answered in that thread. It stays until someone replies in
  the same thread or presses **Handled**. A call or another email on the same contact does not clear it on purpose:
  a reply deserves a reply.
- **Overdue promises.** Tasks and deal next-actions whose date passed. Most overdue first.
- **Decisions coming up.** Deals whose decision deadline or expected close is within the week.

Three scopes at the top: **Mine**, **Unassigned**, **Everyone**. Mine is your work. Unassigned is what nobody owns yet;
**Assign to me** takes it. The counts are exact and the list never hides your items behind someone else's backlog.

## 2. A deal page (where you will spend your time)

Open any deal. The header gives the six facts an AE needs at a glance, each editable in place:

| Fact | What it means |
|---|---|
| Event | Event type, **event date** and venue, city. The production calendar. |
| Buyer | The primary contact or decision maker. Add one below if it says "none yet". |
| Owner | Who is working it. "Unassigned" means nobody. |
| Next action | What happens next and when. Overdue turns red. |
| Expected value | Your estimate and the weighted value (estimate × stage probability). |
| Closing | **Expected close date** and the decision deadline. The sales calendar. |

The two dates are deliberately separate. A commencement in May can be awarded in January: January is when it counts
in the forecast, May is when the trucks roll. If a deal has no expected close date it says so and is in no forecast;
set one as soon as you have a feel for it.

Below the header:

- **Stage bar**: Prospect → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal → Negotiation → Booked. Booked needs the Flex project
  linked (a deal is booked only through Flex). "Mark lost" asks for a reason first.
- **Log a call / note / meeting / email**: type what happened, pick the outcome, optionally create the follow-up task.
  On a phone the big buttons are Call · Note · Task · Email.
- **Focus**: open tasks on the deal, earliest first. The first one is the next step.
- **Sales card**: proposal link and revision, decision deadline, estimated cost and margin, deal type, source.
- **Productions**: one deal can cover several dates or cities (a tour, a two-night show). Add each production with its
  own date, venue and budget. Reporting counts the deal once.
- **History**: everything that happened, filterable by calls, email, tasks.

## 3. Deals list, board and forecast

- **Board**: open deals by stage, drag or use the stage buttons.
- **List**: filter by owner, stage, next action, close month, event month; sort any column; the footer shows the exact
  total. The URL carries your filters, so copy the address bar to share exactly what you see, and Back returns to it.
- **Saved views**: when your filters match no built-in view, "Save view" appears. Personal by default; tick Shared to
  publish it to the team.
- **Select**: a checkbox picks rows on this page; "Select all N matching" picks every row the filter matches, across
  pages. Then Assign, set a stage, or add a task for all of them.
- **Forecast**: by expected close month. Closing this month, already won, and deals with no close date listed apart so
  nothing is guessed.

## 4. Rebooking (Deals → Rebooking)

Past Flex events that are due to come around again, inside the lead window (270 days by default), with no open deal
near the expected date. Each card cites its reasons: the last event, the cadence inferred from the client's history,
the absence of a deal, how long since anyone touched the company. **Assign** it to yourself with a due date before you
do any outreach; **Snooze** pushes it out; **Dismiss** keeps it out. "Create the deal" is an explicit step from the
card once you have talked to them.

## 5. People and Companies

Search, filter, sort and share URLs the same way as deals. A company page shows its Flex history, its people, open
deals and the timeline. "Flex queue" (Deals → Flex queue) is where booked Flex projects are matched to deals.

## 6. Email

One-to-one email and sequences go out through **your** Gmail: Settings → Gmail → Connect Gmail once, consent as
yourself. Replies to anything you sent from FF Tank are detected and land on Today. You can only send as a mailbox you
connected. Campaign email (newsletters, invitations) is a separate flow under Outreach → Campaigns and goes through
Resend, not Gmail.

## 7. Phone

Everything above works at phone width: the deal header stacks, the stage is a select, the four big actions are Call ·
Note · Task · Email, and a deal link someone texts you opens the deal with the dates visible.

## Habits that make the data worth having

1. Log the call or email on the deal, not in your head. The next action and its date are what Today runs on.
2. Set the expected close date when you have a feel for it, and change it when it moves. That is the forecast.
3. Keep the buyer and the owner filled in. Unassigned work is invisible on Mine.
4. Press Handled only when the reply really is handled.
5. Share URLs instead of describing filters.
